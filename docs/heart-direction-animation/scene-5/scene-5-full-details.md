# Scene 5: Full Details

# Overall Behavior

Scene 5 is the reverse journey of Scene 1.

The visitor has completed the interior Heart Direction sequence and arrives at the **Back to Reality** sign, which marks the exit point from the tunnel.

From here, continued scrolling moves the viewer outward through the same heart-shaped tunnel system used at the beginning of the website.

The visual logic should deliberately mirror Scene 1:

- Scene 1 moves inward
- Scene 5 moves outward

The transition should feel spatial and continuous.

The viewer should not feel like the site suddenly changes sections.

They should feel like they physically travel through the final heart opening and emerge into the brighter outside world.

On the other side, the next scene is already waiting:

- closed scroll
- cherubs
- clouds
- bright exterior atmosphere

The closed scroll becomes the new focal point once the viewer exits the tunnel.

---

# Starting Frame

Scene 5 begins with the **Back to Reality** sign fully visible.

At this point:

- the visitor is still inside the tunnel
- the heart-shaped tunnel structure is visible
- the interior environment is still darker
- the exterior daylight world is only faintly visible or completely hidden
- the Back to Reality sign is the primary focal point
- the viewer is facing directly toward the exit

The sign should feel like the final marker inside the symbolic world.

---

# 1. Back to Reality Sign

The Back to Reality sign should remain visually stable at the beginning of the scene.

It is the final piece of language the viewer sees before leaving the tunnel.

## Idle Behavior

The sign should not have exaggerated animation.

Possible subtle behavior:

- soft reflected glow from the heart light carried over from Scene 4
- faint highlight
- gentle brightness breathing
- tiny environmental movement around it

The sign itself should remain readable and grounded.

Avoid:

- bouncing
- large floating
- random rotation
- heavy marquee animation unless the final artwork specifically calls for it

---

# 2. Back to Reality Hold

There should be a short hold before the tunnel exit begins.

This allows the viewer to register:

**I am leaving.**

During this hold:

- Back to Reality remains centered
- tunnel remains stable
- ambient motion continues
- light from Scene 4 remains present
- no major forward camera movement begins immediately

This is the final pause inside the tunnel.

---

# 3. Tunnel Structure

The exit tunnel should use the same layered heart architecture established in Scene 1.

Recommended layers:

- outer heart frame
- inner heart layer 1
- inner heart layer 2
- inner heart layer 3
- tunnel interior
- tunnel lights
- tunnel atmosphere
- final heart opening
- exterior daylight scene beyond opening

The goal is not to create a new tunnel design.

It should visually rhyme with the entrance.

---

# 4. Reverse Tunnel Logic

Scene 5 should reuse the spatial logic of Scene 1, but reverse the direction.

In Scene 1:

- outside world is visible
- visitor approaches heart
- heart grows
- tunnel fills viewport
- viewer moves inward

In Scene 5:

- viewer begins inside tunnel
- visitor approaches exterior opening
- heart layers pass around the camera
- daylight grows
- exterior world fills viewport
- tunnel is left behind

The motion should feel like the same journey retraced in reverse.

---

# 5. Camera Behavior

The camera remains aligned to the center of the heart tunnel.

It should move outward along one central axis.

Avoid:

- side-to-side panning
- rotation
- abrupt shifts
- camera wobble
- unnecessary zooming unrelated to the tunnel geometry

The viewer should always feel oriented toward the exit.

---

# 6. Back to Reality Exit Behavior

As the viewer begins scrolling beyond the hold:

- Back to Reality sign begins moving upward relative to the viewport
- sign gradually leaves the center
- tunnel opening becomes more prominent
- camera begins traveling toward the exit

The sign should not vanish instantly.

It should feel like something the viewer is leaving behind.

---

# 7. Interior Tunnel Lights

The tunnel lights can continue subtle flicker during the exit.

Their behavior should match the visual language used in Scene 1.

Possible behavior:

- mostly illuminated
- occasional dimming
- irregular small-group flicker
- soft warmth

The lights should not distract from the growing daylight ahead.

As the viewer approaches the exit, exterior brightness should gradually overpower the tunnel lights.

---

# 8. Tunnel Light Fade

The interior tunnel should progressively lose visual dominance.

As daylight becomes stronger:

- tunnel bulb contrast decreases
- darker tunnel tones soften
- interior glow becomes less noticeable
- exterior light becomes the strongest source

This should feel like natural exposure change rather than simply reducing opacity on the tunnel.

---

# 9. Heart Layer Movement

The nested heart layers should move past the viewer at slightly different speeds.

This creates depth.

Recommended behavior:

### Nearest Heart Layer

Moves past the camera fastest.

### Middle Heart Layers

Move slightly slower.

### Distant Opening

Moves slowest until it becomes dominant.

The viewer should feel like the camera is physically passing through multiple layers of heart-shaped architecture.

---

# 10. Final Heart Opening

The final opening is the destination.

At first, it may appear:

- small
- bright
- distant
- centered

As the viewer scrolls:

- opening grows
- exterior colors become clearer
- exterior scene becomes more recognizable
- the dark tunnel perimeter moves toward the edges

The final opening should remain centered throughout the transition.

---

# 11. Exterior Daylight Reveal

The outer world should already exist behind the final heart opening.

It should not load after the tunnel disappears.

As the opening grows:

- blue sky becomes visible
- clouds appear
- cherubs become visible
- closed scroll begins to appear
- exterior brightness increases

The opening acts like a mask revealing the outer scene behind it.

This mirrors the entrance logic from Scene 1.

---

# 12. Lighting Shift

Lighting should gradually move from:

**dark interior**

to

**bright exterior**

The change should happen continuously.

Possible progression:

1. dark tunnel
2. bright opening
3. opening grows
4. surrounding tunnel catches exterior light
5. exterior colors become stronger
6. tunnel falls away
7. daylight fills viewport

Avoid a hard flash or abrupt exposure change.

---

# 13. Exterior Background

The outside world should be brighter and more open than the tunnel.

Possible elements:

- blue sky
- subtle texture
- soft atmospheric light
- decorative stars or accents if appropriate
- clouds
- parchment or illustrated environmental detail

The exact artwork may change, but the scene should clearly communicate that the viewer has returned to the outer world.

---

# 14. Exterior Clouds

Clouds should already have subtle ambient motion as they become visible.

They should not wait until the tunnel transition is complete to start moving.

Possible behavior:

- slow horizontal drift
- gentle vertical float
- independent cloud timing
- subtle scale breathing

The cloud system can echo Scene 1 without needing to be identical.

---

# 15. Cherubs

Two cherubs are positioned in the exterior scene around the closed scroll.

They should already be gently animated before the viewer fully exits the tunnel.

This makes the next world feel alive before the camera arrives.

## Idle Animation

Each cherub may:

- float gently
- move slightly vertically
- drift by a few pixels horizontally
- rotate very slightly

Suggested ranges:

- vertical: approximately 4 to 8 pixels
- horizontal: approximately 2 to 5 pixels
- rotation: less than 1 degree
- duration: approximately 4 to 7 seconds

The cherubs should not move in perfect synchronization.

---

# 16. Cherub Character Scope

For the initial implementation, the cherubs do not require full character animation.

No need yet for:

- trumpet movement
- hand movement
- head movement
- facial movement

Optional later enhancements can include:

- subtle trumpet shift
- small wing movement

For now, floating is enough.

---

# 17. Closed Scroll

The closed scroll is the next major story object.

It should already exist in the outer scene before the viewer finishes exiting the tunnel.

The scroll should begin fully closed.

It should not start opening during Scene 5.

Its opening belongs to the next scene.

The purpose here is only to introduce it.

---

# 18. Closed Scroll Starting State

The scroll should be positioned prominently enough that the viewer understands it is the next destination.

At the end of Scene 5:

- scroll is fully closed
- parchment rolls remain tightly wrapped
- no message is visible
- scroll remains centered
- cherubs frame it

The scroll should feel like something waiting to be revealed.

---

# 19. Scroll Idle Behavior

The closed scroll should remain mostly still.

Possible subtle idle motion:

- very small vertical float
- tiny shadow shift
- slight parchment texture movement

Avoid:

- partial opening
- large rotation
- bounce
- dramatic movement

The next scene needs room to make the unfurling meaningful.

---

# 20. Exterior Scene Arrival

As the viewer clears the final heart opening:

- tunnel architecture passes beyond viewport edges
- Back to Reality is fully gone
- daylight fills the screen
- cherubs are visible
- clouds frame the scene
- closed scroll becomes central

The camera should gradually slow.

The motion should settle naturally rather than stopping abruptly.

---

# 21. Exterior Scene Hold

Once the viewer is fully outside, there should be a short hold.

During this hold:

- scroll remains closed
- cherubs float
- clouds drift
- exterior atmosphere continues
- no scroll unfurling begins yet

This creates a clean boundary between:

**exiting the tunnel**

and

**opening the proclamation**

---

# 22. Visual Attention Hierarchy

At the beginning:

1. Back to Reality sign
2. tunnel opening
3. exterior light beyond

During tunnel exit:

1. growing daylight opening
2. tunnel layers passing around camera
3. exterior scene becoming visible

At the end:

1. closed scroll
2. cherubs
3. clouds
4. exterior atmosphere

The focus should clearly hand off from exit marker to scroll.

---

# 23. Ambient Animation Hierarchy

## Tunnel Portion

Primary:

- subtle tunnel light flicker

Secondary:

- atmospheric movement

Mostly still:

- Back to Reality sign

## Exterior Portion

Primary:

- cherub floating
- cloud drifting

Secondary:

- subtle atmospheric texture

Mostly still:

- closed scroll

---

# 24. Layer Structure

Recommended logical order:

### Tunnel Background

1. Interior tunnel environment
2. tunnel atmosphere
3. tunnel light effects

### Back to Reality

1. Back to Reality sign
2. sign highlight/glow

### Tunnel Architecture

1. outer heart layer
2. middle heart layer
3. inner heart layer
4. final opening mask

### Exterior Scene

1. exterior sky
2. background texture
3. exterior atmosphere
4. rear clouds
5. front clouds
6. left cherub
7. right cherub
8. closed scroll
9. optional scroll shadow/highlight

The exterior scene should sit behind the final tunnel opening so it is revealed progressively.

---

# 25. Tunnel Masking

The heart-shaped opening should act as the reveal mask for the exterior scene.

As the viewer exits:

- opening grows
- more exterior scene becomes visible
- tunnel edges pass beyond the viewport

The viewer should never see a rectangular boundary around the outer scene.

Possible implementation:

- CSS clip-path
- SVG mask
- nested overflow container
- layered heart frame

The exact method should prioritize smooth mobile performance.

---

# 26. Scroll Behavior

Scene 5 should remain pinned during the tunnel exit.

The scroll controls:

- Back to Reality leaving
- camera movement through the tunnel
- heart-layer scaling
- daylight reveal
- exterior scene arrival
- final settling hold

Ambient animation remains time-based.

---

# 27. Scroll Reversal

Scrolling upward should cleanly reverse the tunnel exit.

If the visitor reverses:

- exterior opening shrinks
- tunnel architecture returns around the camera
- cherubs and scroll recede behind the opening
- interior tunnel becomes dominant again
- Back to Reality returns

The viewer should feel like they are moving back into the tunnel.

No visual reset should occur.

---

# 28. Relationship to Scene 1

Scene 5 should intentionally reuse Scene 1’s tunnel logic where possible.

This can include:

- same heart layer structure
- same masking approach
- same center alignment
- same relative depth logic
- same performance strategy

The main difference is direction.

Reusing the system is preferable to building a completely separate exit animation.

---

# 29. Mobile Behavior

Mobile portrait is the master layout.

The heart opening must remain centered as browser UI changes height.

Use dynamic viewport units where appropriate.

The exterior scene should have enough bleed around the edges so scaling never reveals empty space.

Cherubs and scroll must remain within safe viewing zones.

---

# 30. Mobile Exterior Composition

The closed scroll should fit comfortably in portrait view.

Cherubs should frame it without pushing important content outside the visible region.

Clouds may crop naturally at the sides.

The exterior scene should not feel cramped after the spacious tunnel exit.

---

# 31. Desktop Behavior

Desktop preserves the same choreography.

Wider screens may reveal:

- more clouds
- more sky
- more negative space around the cherubs

Do not change the scene into a horizontal layout.

The scroll remains the central destination.

---

# 32. Performance Requirements

Reuse the Scene 1 tunnel system where possible.

Prefer:

- transform
- scale
- opacity
- pre-rendered lighting layers
- optimized image assets

Avoid:

- large real-time blur calculations
- rebuilding layout every scroll frame
- duplicate tunnel timelines
- unnecessary DOM complexity

The exterior scene should be preloaded before the viewer begins the exit transition.

---

# 33. Reduced Motion

For visitors using `prefers-reduced-motion`:

- shorten the tunnel travel
- reduce layered parallax
- crossfade more directly from interior to exterior
- keep Back to Reality readable
- preserve closed-scroll reveal
- reduce cherub/cloud motion

The narrative order should remain:

Back to Reality → exit tunnel → exterior world → closed scroll.

---

# 34. Placeholder Artwork Requirements

Before final artwork is complete, the scene should at minimum have separate assets for:

- Back to Reality sign
- outer heart tunnel layer
- middle heart tunnel layer
- inner heart tunnel layer
- tunnel light overlay
- final heart mask/opening
- exterior sky
- cloud groups
- left cherub
- right cherub
- closed scroll

The most important mechanics to test early are:

- reverse tunnel movement
- masking
- exterior reveal
- cherub/scroll placement
- scroll reversal

---

# 35. Locked Scene Behavior

When Scene 5 begins:

- Back to Reality is visible
- visitor remains inside the tunnel
- tunnel exit lies ahead
- continued scrolling moves camera outward
- Back to Reality moves away
- heart layers pass around the viewer
- daylight grows
- exterior scene becomes visible through the opening
- cherubs and closed scroll are already present beyond the tunnel
- viewer fully exits
- tunnel disappears behind
- closed scroll becomes the primary focal point
- exterior scene holds
- next scene begins from the closed-scroll composition

---

# Acceptance Criteria

Scene 5 is complete when:

1. Back to Reality is clearly visible at the beginning.
2. The scene begins inside the tunnel.
3. Tunnel exit visually mirrors Scene 1 in reverse.
4. Viewer feels like the camera is moving outward.
5. Tunnel remains centered.
6. Back to Reality leaves naturally as the viewer exits.
7. Heart layers move past the camera with depth.
8. Exterior daylight is visible behind the final opening before the tunnel disappears.
9. Exterior scene is revealed through the heart-shaped opening.
10. No rectangular image edges become visible.
11. Lighting transitions smoothly from dark interior to bright exterior.
12. Tunnel lights become visually secondary as daylight increases.
13. Clouds are already moving when they become visible.
14. Cherubs are already floating when revealed.
15. Closed scroll is already present outside the tunnel.
16. Scroll remains fully closed throughout Scene 5.
17. Scroll does not begin unfurling early.
18. Viewer fully exits the tunnel before the next major animation begins.
19. Exterior scene has a short settling hold.
20. Closed scroll becomes the primary focal point.
21. Cherubs remain secondary to the scroll.
22. Reverse scrolling recreates the inward tunnel journey cleanly.
23. Back to Reality returns correctly during reverse scroll.
24. Tunnel masking remains stable at all scroll speeds.
25. Scene performs smoothly on mobile.
26. Mobile browser height changes do not break alignment.
27. Final artwork can replace placeholders without rebuilding the animation system.
28. Scene cleanly hands off into the next scene with the **closed scroll ready to open**.
