# Scene 3: Transition

# **Transition Goal**

The transition out of Scene 3 continues the arrow’s downward journey.

After the arrow has completed its initial shot behind the three medallions and the medallion scene has had time to settle, continued scrolling moves the arrow farther down the page toward the next major symbol: the heart.

The arrow should travel only far enough for its tip to enter the heart.

Once it reaches the heart, part of the arrow disappears behind the heart artwork so it looks like the arrow has physically struck it.

That impact activates the next animated beat:

**the heart lights up.**

The arrow should feel like the continuous story object connecting the medallions to the heart.

# **Starting State**

The transition begins after Scene 3 has fully settled.

Visible:

- Medallion 1
- Medallion 2
- Medallion 3
- arrow resting behind all three medallions
- surrounding clouds
- animated gold glow around the medallions

The arrow is stationary.

The visitor has already had time to interact with the medallions.

Continued scrolling begins the transition.

# **Phase 1: Arrow Resumes Its Journey**

As the visitor scrolls down, the arrow begins moving downward again.

This should feel like a continuation of the same arrow flight, not a new arrow animation.

The medallions remain behind as the arrow moves away from them.

The arrow should:

- stay vertically centered
- continue moving straight downward
- maintain the same orientation
- remain visually consistent with the arrow used in the previous scenes

There should be no spinning, swaying, or horizontal drift.

The arrow is moving with purpose toward the heart below.

# **Phase 2: Medallions Leave the Frame**

As the arrow moves downward, the medallion composition gradually moves upward and out of the viewport.

The three medallions do not need to dramatically animate away.

They are simply being left behind as the scroll journey continues.

The viewer’s attention should shift from:

**Medallions**

to

**Arrow**

to

**Heart**

The medallion glow can continue while they remain visible and stop once they are fully offscreen.

# **Phase 3: Heart Enters the Viewport**

As scrolling continues, the heart begins appearing from below.

The arrow remains centered directly above it.

The heart should feel like the arrow’s destination.

The composition should clearly establish the alignment:

**Arrow**

↓

**Heart**

The arrow should not have to reposition horizontally to hit the heart.

The heart should already be centered beneath the arrow’s path.

# **Phase 4: Arrow Approaches the Heart**

The arrow continues moving downward toward the heart.

As the gap closes, the movement can slow slightly so the impact is readable.

The arrow should remain fully visible until the arrowhead reaches the heart.

The viewer should clearly understand:

**The arrow is about to hit this object.**

No major heart animation should begin before contact.

# **Phase 5: Arrow Strikes the Heart**

The arrow reaches the heart.

The arrowhead and a small portion of the shaft should move behind the heart artwork.

This creates the illusion that the arrow has physically entered the heart.

The arrow should not simply stop on top of the heart image.

The layering should visually communicate penetration.

The result should look like:

**visible arrow shaft**

↓

**arrow enters heart**

↓

**arrow tip disappears behind heart**

The heart remains in front of the section of arrow that has entered it.

# **Arrow Layering at Impact**

Before impact:

Background

↓

Heart

↓

Arrow moving toward heart

At the exact impact point, the layering or mask should change so the arrow appears to enter the heart.

The final visual relationship should be:

Background

↓

Arrow section behind heart

↓

Heart

↓

Arrow shaft still visible above heart

This may require:

- masking
- clip-path
- multiple arrow layers
- or a dedicated impact mask

The simplest reliable implementation should be used.

# **Phase 6: Arrow Stops**

Once the arrow has entered the heart, it stops moving.

It should remain lodged there.

The arrow should not:

- bounce back
- recoil
- continue through the heart
- disappear completely
- wobble dramatically

The impact should feel clean and intentional.

A tiny settling movement could be used if needed, but it should be extremely subtle.

# **Phase 7: Heart Activation**

The moment the arrow strikes, the heart lights up.

This is the immediate visual consequence of the impact.

The heart should transition from its resting state into an illuminated state.

The activation can include:

- heart becoming brighter
- warm internal glow appearing
- outer glow expanding
- surrounding rays becoming brighter
- light briefly blooming outward

The heart should feel activated, not damaged.

The arrow hitting it triggers illumination.

# **Heart Light Sequence**

Recommended sequence:

1. Arrow enters heart
2. Very brief impact pause
3. Heart rapidly brightens
4. Inner glow expands
5. Rays/light accents become visible or brighter
6. Outer halo blooms
7. Heart settles into an illuminated resting state

The activation should be faster than the normal ambient glow animations used elsewhere on the site.

It is a reaction.

# **Heart Resting State After Impact**

Once activated, the heart should remain visibly illuminated.

It should not immediately return to its original unlit state.

Possible resting behavior:

- steady internal glow
- gentle breathing brightness
- subtle outer halo
- occasional small pulse

This illuminated heart becomes the starting state for the next scene.

# **Cloud Behavior**

Clouds can continue framing the transition as the heart enters the viewport.

They should remain secondary.

Possible behavior:

- upper clouds move out with the medallions
- lower clouds frame the arriving heart
- subtle ambient drift continues

The clouds should not obscure:

- the arrow tip
- the impact point
- the heart itself

# **Camera Behavior**

The camera remains vertically aligned with the arrow.

There should be no dramatic camera movement.

The scroll itself creates the downward travel.

The viewer should feel like they are following the arrow through the story.

As the heart enters, the camera simply reveals more of the environment below.

# **Scroll Behavior**

The transition should remain scrubbed to scroll until the arrow reaches the heart.

That means:

- slow scrolling produces slow arrow movement
- faster scrolling produces faster arrow movement
- reverse scrolling moves the arrow back upward toward the medallions

Once the arrow crosses the impact point, the heart illumination can behave as a triggered animation.

This allows the actual impact to feel more immediate than a slow scrubbed glow.

# **Reverse Scroll Behavior**

If the visitor scrolls upward before the arrow reaches the heart:

- arrow moves back upward
- heart moves out of view
- medallions return naturally

If the visitor reverses after the impact:

- arrow should visually pull back out of the heart
- heart illumination should return toward its pre-impact state
- scene should reconnect cleanly with the medallion composition

The exact reverse behavior of the light should be tuned so it does not look broken or flash abruptly.

# **Transition Rhythm**

The transition should read:

**Medallions settled**

↓

**Scroll**

↓

**Arrow begins moving downward**

↓

**Medallions are left behind**

↓

**Heart enters from below**

↓

**Arrow approaches heart**

↓

**Arrow tip enters heart**

↓

**Arrow stops**

↓

**HEART LIGHTS UP**

↓

**Heart remains illuminated**

↓

**Next scene begins**

# **Ending State**

At the end of the transition:

- medallions are no longer the focal point
- arrow is lodged inside the heart
- part of the arrow is hidden behind the heart
- heart is fully illuminated
- heart glow is active
- clouds frame the new composition
- the viewer is ready to enter the next animated scene

This exact illuminated-heart composition becomes the opening state of the next scene.
