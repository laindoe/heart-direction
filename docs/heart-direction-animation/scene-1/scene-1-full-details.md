# Scene 1: Full Details

# Overall Behavior

The entire composition should load together as one complete illustrated scene.

Nothing should dramatically enter, drop in, fade in, or assemble on page load.

The viewer should immediately see:

- the sky
- background atmosphere
- clouds
- decorative stars and sparkles
- title signage
- cherubs
- banners
- heart tunnel
- runway
- Enter the Tunnel button

Once the complete composition is visible, subtle ambient movement begins.

The opening frame should feel alive, but mostly settled.

The heart tunnel is the visual destination. The viewer should immediately understand:

**That opening is where I am going.**

The opening scene remains in this living idle state until the viewer begins scrolling or activates the Enter the Tunnel button.

---

# Starting Frame

When the page first loads, the entire opening composition is visible at once.

The scene should already feel finished before the visitor interacts.

The composition should be designed for a vertical mobile viewport first.

No important text, character, button, or visual element should sit beneath the browser interface, home indicator, or mobile navigation controls.

The heart tunnel should remain centrally positioned and act as the primary visual anchor.

---

# 1. Background Sky

## Visual Structure

The background should be divided into at least three visual layers:

1. Base blue sky
2. Texture layer
3. Small atmospheric details

The base blue sky provides the stable foundation of the scene.

The texture and atmospheric layers can eventually contain:

- paper grain
- faint painted variation
- soft light patches
- tiny stars
- sparkles
- barely visible drifting haze
- other subtle background details

## Idle Animation

The base blue sky should remain still.

The texture layer can move extremely slowly and almost imperceptibly.

Possible movement:

- slight horizontal drift
- subtle opacity breathing
- very gentle scale movement

The movement should be quiet enough that the viewer notices atmosphere rather than consciously noticing the background animation.

### Suggested Motion

- Drift approximately 1% to 2%
- Duration approximately 15 to 25 seconds
- Alternate direction continuously
- No obvious looping jump

## Important

Do not animate one giant flattened background image if possible.

The base sky, texture, stars, and other atmospheric elements should exist independently so movement can occur without shifting the entire composition.

---

# 2. Clouds

The clouds are one of the primary ambient-motion elements in the opening scene.

The current composition contains several different cloud areas:

- upper-left cloud mass
- upper-right cloud mass
- side clouds surrounding the heart
- lower clouds surrounding the runway
- clouds partially behind the cherubs

These should not all move at the same speed or in the same direction.

Different cloud behavior creates visual depth and prevents the environment from looking like one flat image sliding across the screen.

## Far-Background Clouds

Behavior:

- move slowly from left to right
- very small travel distance
- longest animation duration

These clouds should provide the slowest layer of environmental motion.

## Middle-Ground Clouds

Behavior:

- move right to left
- slightly faster than the far-background clouds
- remain subtle

The opposite direction helps separate the visual planes.

## Foreground Clouds

Behavior:

- move only a few pixels horizontally
- gently rise and fall
- may subtly scale between approximately 1 and 1.02

These should feel closer to the viewer without becoming distracting.

## Loop Behavior

Cloud motion should be seamless and continuous.

Avoid:

- clouds traveling completely offscreen and suddenly reappearing
- every cloud moving simultaneously
- identical animation durations
- identical directions
- exaggerated cartoon speed
- visible jumps when loops restart

The result should feel like a moving painted sky.

## Important

Cloud assets should never expose empty blue gaps where the illustration ends.

Each moving cloud asset should either contain additional illustrated area beyond the visible portion or remain within a small enough movement range that its edges never become visible.

---

# 3. Decorative Stars and Sparkles

Stars and sparkles should provide occasional points of life throughout the composition.

Not every star should animate.

Choose approximately three to six accent stars or small star groups to animate.

## Idle Animation

Individual stars may:

- brighten
- scale up slightly
- rotate a few degrees
- fade back to their resting state

Their timing should be irregular.

Some stars should remain completely still so the scene remains visually grounded.

Avoid synchronized twinkling.

The viewer should not be able to immediately recognize a repeating pattern.

---

# 4. Left Cherub

The left cherub should feel suspended in the air rather than attached to the composition.

## Idle Animation

The cherub should:

- gently float upward and downward
- drift slightly inward and outward
- rotate by a very small amount

Suggested behavior:

- rise slightly while drifting inward
- rotate a fraction clockwise
- slowly return to the original position

### Suggested Range

- Vertical movement: approximately 4 to 10 pixels
- Horizontal movement: approximately 2 to 6 pixels
- Rotation: less than 1 degree
- Duration: approximately 4 to 7 seconds

The movement should loop smoothly.

---

# 5. Right Cherub

The right cherub should use the same overall floating language as the left cherub, but the animation should not be mirrored exactly.

Suggested behavior:

- begin the loop at a different time
- lower slightly while drifting outward
- rotate a fraction counterclockwise
- slowly return

### Suggested Range

- Vertical movement: approximately 4 to 10 pixels
- Horizontal movement: approximately 2 to 6 pixels
- Rotation: less than 1 degree
- Duration: approximately 4 to 7 seconds

The two cherubs should use different durations and offsets so they never appear synchronized.

They should feel related, but independent.

## Character Animation Scope

The cherubs do not need full character animation for this version.

Do not currently animate:

- wings
- hands
- bows
- facial expressions
- individual body parts

Possible future enhancements could include:

- slight wing flutter
- tiny bow tension
- subtle hand movement

For the current animation system, floating alone is enough.

---

# 6. “Introducing Lain Doe Love” Banner

The top banner functions as part of the title hierarchy.

It should remain structurally stable and should not compete with the heart tunnel or cherubs.

## Idle Animation

Possible subtle treatments:

- faint warm highlight traveling across the parchment
- extremely gentle paper movement
- tiny shadow shift
- occasional soft light pass

The recommended treatment is an infrequent soft light pass every several seconds.

The banner itself should not significantly bob, bounce, rotate, or move around the composition.

---

# 7. “The Heart Director” Title

The Heart Director title should be one of the most stable elements in the entire opening scene.

It functions as the identity stamp of the page.

## Idle Animation

Keep the actual letterforms still.

Only supporting effects should animate.

Possible effects:

- slight gold shimmer
- tiny glint on selected edges
- faint shadow breathing
- occasional nearby star sparkle

Do not continuously sweep a highlight across the entire title.

Instead, small areas should occasionally catch light independently.

The title should remain readable and visually stable at all times.

---

# 8. “Revealing the Invisible” Banner

The lower banner functions as the subtitle or thesis statement beneath the Heart Director title.

It can have slightly more movement than the top banner while remaining restrained.

## Idle Animation

Possible movement:

- gentle float of approximately 1 to 3 pixels
- subtle parchment ripple
- small highlight catching one side of the banner

The text must remain readable at all times.

Do not bend, distort, or warp the banner enough to affect the typography.

---

# 9. Heart Tunnel

The heart tunnel is the primary visual anchor of Scene 1.

Before the visitor begins scrolling, the structure itself should remain mostly stationary.

It should not bob or visibly float.

The tunnel can feel alive through lighting and extremely subtle depth changes rather than physical movement.

## Tunnel Structure

The tunnel should be separated into independent visual layers.

Recommended structure:

- outer heart architecture
- red marquee light overlay
- inner black-and-gold heart layer 1
- inner black-and-gold heart layer 2
- inner black-and-gold heart layer 3
- distant inner opening
- tunnel interior
- central glow
- runway

These layers will later allow the transition animation to create depth rather than simply scaling one flattened tunnel image.

## Ambient Movement

The heart architecture itself should remain stable.

An extremely small depth-breathing effect may be used if needed.

Suggested range:

- Scale from 1 to approximately 1.005
- Duration approximately 4 to 6 seconds
- Smooth return

The effect should be almost invisible.

The tunnel should feel alive without appearing to physically wobble.

---

# 10. Heart Tunnel Lights

The bulbs surrounding the heart should behave like slightly imperfect theatrical or vintage marquee lights.

They should not all blink simultaneously.

## Flicker Groups

Divide the bulbs into small animation groups.

For example:

**Group A**

Outer-left bulbs

**Group B**

Outer-right bulbs

**Group C**

Top bulbs

**Group D**

Inner-heart bulbs

**Group E**

Occasional individual bulbs

Each group should use slightly different timing.

## Flicker Behavior

Most bulbs should remain illuminated most of the time.

Individual bulbs or small groups may:

- dim briefly
- brighten again
- occasionally flutter twice
- vary slightly in intensity

### Suggested Brightness Pattern

Normal state:

**85% to 95% brightness**

Occasional dim:

**45% to 70% brightness**

Flickers should be brief with irregular pauses between them.

## Avoid

- full blackouts
- every bulb blinking together
- fast strobe effects
- constant chaotic flickering
- synchronized pulsing
- obvious repeating sequences

The effect should resemble slightly imperfect vintage carnival or theater lighting.

## Scroll Enhancement

Once scrolling begins, the lights may briefly illuminate inward in sequence to guide the viewer deeper into the tunnel.

That behavior belongs to the **Transition page**, not the idle animation.

---

# 11. Tunnel Interior Glow

The distant center of the tunnel should softly brighten and dim.

This provides another subtle visual cue pulling the viewer toward the heart opening.

## Idle Animation

The glow should:

- slowly brighten
- slowly soften
- subtly shift in warmth if desired
- operate independently from the marquee bulbs

Avoid harsh flashing.

The glow should feel inviting and magnetic.

---

# 12. Runway

The runway visually directs the viewer toward the heart tunnel.

During the opening idle state, the runway should remain structurally still.

## Optional Ambient Effects

Possible subtle effects:

- faint highlight moving toward the tunnel
- small glow collecting near the distant end
- very slight texture movement

Do not move the entire runway before scrolling begins.

The illusion of forward movement belongs to the scroll transition.

---

# 13. Enter the Tunnel Button

The Enter the Tunnel button is the strongest explicit invitation in the scene.

It can therefore have slightly more noticeable ambient animation than the banners or title.

The button should still feel integrated into the illustrated world rather than behaving like standard website UI.

## Button Frame

The sign itself remains stable.

It should not bounce, float dramatically, or continuously scale.

---

# 14. Enter the Tunnel Button Lights

The bulbs around the button should use the same visual language as the heart tunnel bulbs.

Because this is an interactive element, the animation can be slightly more prominent.

Possible behavior:

- gentle pulse
- irregular flickering in small groups
- occasional short chase around the frame
- increased brightness when touched or hovered

The light behavior should make the button feel interactive without becoming distracting.

---

# 15. Button Heart Ornament

The central heart ornament above the button can slowly glow or pulse.

This should be softer and slower than the marquee flicker.

It acts as a small visual echo of the heart tunnel.

# Button Interaction

The visitor should be able to begin the journey in either of two ways:

1. Scroll naturally
2. Tap the Enter the Tunnel button

When the button is tapped:

- the button bulbs brighten
- the central heart flashes softly
- the page smoothly advances into the first scroll-controlled portion of the tunnel transition

The button should not trigger a completely separate animation.

It should simply advance the same scroll journey the visitor would enter naturally.

---

# Opening Animation Hierarchy

Not every element should demand equal visual attention.

The animation hierarchy should be:

## Most Visible Ambient Movement

- cherubs floating
- selected clouds drifting
- Enter the Tunnel button lights flickering
- heart tunnel lights flickering

## Secondary Ambient Movement

- subtle title shimmer
- selected stars twinkling
- central tunnel glow
- faint parchment highlights
- background atmospheric movement

## Mostly Still

- Heart Director title
- runway
- major heart structure
- primary banner positions
- button structure

This hierarchy prevents the opening scene from becoming visually busy.

# Layer Structure

Scene 1 should be constructed from independent layers rather than one flattened image. The existing technical plan explicitly calls for separate exterior, portal, tunnel, atmosphere, and destination layers so the artwork can later be replaced without rebuilding the animation system.

Recommended logical layer order:

### Exterior Background

- base sky
- background texture
- atmospheric details

### Exterior Middle Ground

- rear clouds
- stars
- environmental elements

### Exterior Foreground

- foreground clouds
- cherubs

### Title Architecture

- Introducing Lain Doe Love banner
- Heart Director title
- Revealing the Invisible banner

### Portal Architecture

- outer heart structure
- marquee lights
- inner heart layers

### Ground

- runway

### Interactive Foreground

- Enter the Tunnel button
- button bulbs
- heart ornament

### Tunnel Interior

- interior environment
- atmospheric layer
- central glow
- distant destination

Each layer should be independently replaceable when the final artwork is created.

# Camera Behavior

During the idle state, the camera remains stationary.

Once scrolling begins, the camera should behave as though it is moving forward along one central axis toward the heart opening.

The camera should remain centered on the tunnel.

Avoid:

- unnecessary side-to-side camera movement
- excessive rotation
- sudden changes in scale speed
- large blur effects
- bouncing or elastic easing
- movement that reveals rectangular asset edges

Any parallax should support the illusion of forward travel.

The transition itself is documented separately in **Scene 1: Transition**.

---

# Scroll Behavior

Scene 1 should use scrubbed scroll animation.

Animation progress should remain directly connected to scroll position.

Recommended behavior:

- smooth scrub with slight delay, approximately 0.6 to 1
- no autoplay
- no forced snapping during the entrance
- reverse cleanly when the visitor scrolls upward
- keep the scene pinned while the transition plays
- work correctly at slow, medium, and fast scroll speeds

The visitor should be able to stop at any point without seeing broken layers or unfinished states.

---

# Mobile Behavior

Mobile is the primary layout.

The composition should account for:

- narrow portrait screens
- one-handed scrolling
- changing browser bar heights
- dynamic viewport units such as `svh` or `dvh`
- safe-area padding
- smooth iPhone performance

The heart entrance must remain centered even when viewport height changes as mobile browser controls appear or disappear.

The tunnel artwork should extend far enough beyond the screen edges that scaling never exposes empty background.

Typography must remain legible and unobstructed.

The original technical specification explicitly establishes mobile as the master layout rather than a secondary adaptation.

---

# Desktop Behavior

Desktop should preserve the same choreography.

It should not become a separate experience.

On wider screens:

- reveal additional horizontal scenery where appropriate
- keep the portal centrally framed
- slightly increase parallax distance if beneficial
- calibrate scale values independently when necessary
- ensure the tunnel still completely fills the viewport during entry

Mobile remains the master composition.

---

# Performance Requirements

Use transform and opacity for most animation.

Avoid changing layout properties during scrolling.

Preload critical opening and tunnel assets.

Compress large artwork.

Use WebP or AVIF where practical.

Avoid animating extremely large transparent canvases unless necessary.

Apply `will-change` only to actively animated layers.

Do not create an independent scroll listener for every object.

Control the scene through the master GSAP timeline.

Refresh ScrollTrigger after fonts and critical images load.

---

# Reduced Motion

For visitors using `prefers-reduced-motion`:

- remove the extended forward zoom
- simplify the transition into a crossfade or shorter movement
- preserve the narrative order
- maintain text legibility
- avoid forcing the visitor through a lengthy pinned animation

The story should remain understandable even when the full motion system is disabled.

---

# Placeholder Artwork

The current artwork can remain during animation development.

Final illustration should not be required before the animation system is tested.

However, temporary artwork needs enough independent layers to accurately test the intended motion.

At minimum:

- exterior scene
- heart portal frame
- tunnel interior
- introductory typography
- next-scene destination

The animation system should not depend on visual details that may disappear when the final artwork is created.

---

# Locked Opening State

When the website loads:

- the full composition appears immediately
- clouds drift at varied speeds
- cherubs float gently and independently
- selected stars twinkle occasionally
- the tunnel glows softly
- tunnel bulbs flicker in irregular groups
- Enter the Tunnel bulbs flicker slightly more prominently
- title and banners remain largely stable
- the central tunnel opening subtly draws the eye inward
- scrolling or tapping Enter the Tunnel begins the forward journey

This is the locked idle state for Scene 1.

---

# Acceptance Criteria

The Full Detail portion of Scene 1 is successful when:

- the complete opening composition appears immediately
- no major object visibly assembles itself on load
- ambient movement begins naturally after loading
- clouds move independently without revealing asset edges
- cherubs float independently without synchronized loops
- marquee lights flicker organically without strobing
- tunnel glow remains subtle and inviting
- typography remains visually stable
- runway remains stationary before scroll
- button clearly feels interactive
- the scene remains visually alive when the visitor stops scrolling
- all moving objects exist as independently controllable layers
- mobile composition remains stable as browser UI changes
- final artwork can replace temporary assets without requiring animation architecture changes
- the scene is technically prepared to hand off into the separate **Scene 1: Transition** choreography
