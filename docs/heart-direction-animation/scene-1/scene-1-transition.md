# Scene 1: Transition

# **Scene 1B: The Scroll Begins**

**Starting point**

At the beginning of the scroll:

- the full opening composition is still visible
- the cherubs are floating
- the clouds are drifting
- the heart lights and button lights are flickering
- the tunnel opening is still relatively small
- the viewer has not moved yet

As soon as the user scrolls, the ambient animations continue, but the scene begins responding to scroll progress.

# **Phase 1: The invitation releases**

During the first small portion of the scroll, the opening scene should begin clearing the way.

**What happens**

- the **Enter the Tunnel button** starts moving downward and fading
- the **two banners and title signage** begin moving upward very slightly
- the **cherubs drift outward toward the edges**
- the **clouds separate more clearly**, opening space around the heart
- the **heart tunnel begins enlarging**
- the **runway starts stretching toward the viewer**

This first movement should be gentle. The scene is loosening its grip before the actual plunge.

**Suggested timing**

About the first **10% to 20%** of the entrance animation.

# **Phase 2: The heart becomes the entire destination**

Now the tunnel takes control of the frame.

**Heart tunnel**

The heart tunnel scales up from its center.

Its position should remain locked so the small inner opening stays visually centered.

The important part is that the heart tunnel should be separated from the rest of the composition. The title, cherubs, clouds, and button should not enlarge at the same rate.

**Runway**

The runway should appear to rush beneath the camera.

This can be done by:

- scaling it faster than the heart
- moving its lower edge beyond the bottom of the screen
- increasing perspective
- slightly stretching it vertically

The runway creates the strongest illusion that the viewer is traveling forward.

**Cherubs**

The cherubs should move outward and slightly backward.

They can:

- drift toward the left and right edges
- scale down a little
- fade slightly
- continue floating while leaving the scene

They should not fly dramatically away. They are being left behind as the viewer enters.

**Title and banners**

The title architecture should move upward and out of frame.

The hierarchy could be:

1. “Introducing Lain Doe Love” leaves first
2. “The Heart Director” follows
3. “Revealing the Invisible” remains slightly longer
4. all signage disappears before the tunnel fills the viewport

This lets the identity register before the visual world takes over.

**Clouds**

The clouds should move in opposing directions to open the portal.

For example:

- left clouds move left
- right clouds move right
- lower clouds move downward
- some distant clouds remain visible behind the heart slightly longer

This creates the feeling that the sky is parting.

**Suggested timing**

Roughly **20% to 55%** of the entrance sequence.

# **Phase 3: Crossing the heart threshold**

This is the most important part.

The outer heart grows until its edges pass beyond the screen.

At this point, the viewer should no longer see the complete heart shape. They are too close to it.

**What happens**

- the outer red and black heart layers move beyond the viewport
- the lights become larger as they pass the camera
- the runway disappears beneath the viewer
- the exterior sky is almost completely gone
- the tunnel interior behind the heart becomes dominant
- the camera continues forward toward the small inner opening

The scene should not fade to black or cut.

The artwork should pass around the camera.

**Lighting behavior**

As the viewer crosses:

- the marquee bulbs can brighten briefly
- a short inward chase of light can travel toward the tunnel center
- the outer world becomes dimmer
- the central tunnel glow becomes stronger

This gives the transition a ceremonial little “you may enter” moment.

**Suggested timing**

Roughly **55% to 80%**.

# **Phase 4: Inside the tunnel**

Once the outer heart passes beyond the viewport, the screen becomes the interior tunnel.

The outside title scene is now completely gone.

**What remains visible**

- repeating heart layers surrounding the viewer
- the dark tunnel interior
- small lights or stars
- the distant opening ahead
- subtle tunnel texture

**Camera movement**

The camera continues moving forward, but more slowly than before.

The sudden acceleration of the entrance should ease into a controlled glide.

The repeating heart layers can move past the camera at different speeds to create depth.

For example:

- nearest heart layer moves fastest
- middle heart layers move moderately
- distant opening moves slowly
- small stars drift at varied speeds

**Ambient animation**

The heart lights can keep flickering, but their behavior should feel more directional inside the tunnel.

Some bulbs could activate from outer layer to inner layer, creating a path forward.

**Suggested timing**

Roughly **80% to 100%**.

# **Ending frame**

At the end of Scene 1B:

- the viewer is fully inside the tunnel
- no exterior title elements remain
- the tunnel fills the whole screen
- the camera is facing forward
- the next scene appears as a small image or destination at the end of the tunnel
- motion settles briefly before Scene 2 begins

This final frame should match the first frame of the next scene perfectly.

# **Element-by-element exit map**

| **Element** | **What happens after scrolling begins** |
| --- | --- |
| Background sky | Gradually covered and moves beyond frame |
| Texture | Continues subtly until exterior disappears |
| Clouds | Part sideways and downward |
| Cherubs | Drift outward, scale down slightly, fade |
| Top banner | Moves upward and fades first |
| Heart Director title | Moves upward and fades |
| Lower banner | Leaves slightly later |
| Heart tunnel | Scales forward until edges pass camera |
| Tunnel bulbs | Flicker, then briefly brighten during crossing |
| Runway | Rushes beneath viewer and exits bottom |
| Enter button | Moves down and fades quickly |
| Stars | Some remain, others transition into tunnel stars |
| Central opening | Grows gradually and becomes next focal point |

# **Important technical note**

The tunnel should probably not be one single flat image.

For the best effect, it should be built from several nested heart layers:

Outer heart frame

Heart layer 2

Heart layer 3

Heart layer 4

Distant opening

Each layer can scale at a slightly different rate. That produces real depth without needing full 3D.

The animation should be reversible too. When the user scrolls upward, every element should return cleanly to the exact opening tableau.
