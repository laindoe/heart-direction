# Scene 6: Overview

## Purpose

Scene 6 reveals the Ten Grand proclamation as the final major message after the visitor exits the Heart Direction tunnel.

The scene begins in the bright exterior world with:

- blue sky
- clouds
- floating cherubs
- a fully closed parchment scroll

As the visitor scrolls, the parchment physically unfurls.

At first, the viewport remains pinned and the scroll itself expands downward inside the frame.

Once the parchment grows taller than the viewport, the visitor begins traveling down the proclamation as it continues to open.

After the full message has been revealed, continued scrolling causes the parchment to roll closed again.

The closed scroll then clears the frame and makes way for the final scene.

---

## Narrative Goal

Scene 6 has three primary story beats:

1. **Open the proclamation**
2. **Read the full declaration**
3. **Close the proclamation and move into the finale**

The visitor should feel like they are physically opening and reading a ceremonial announcement rather than simply scrolling through a long image.

The scroll is the mechanism that controls the pacing of the message.

The progression should read:

**Closed scroll**

↓

**Scroll opens**

↓

**Message is revealed**

↓

**Visitor travels down the proclamation**

↓

**Full declaration is reached**

↓

**Scroll closes**

↓

**Final scene emerges**

---

## Starting State

Scene 6 begins immediately after the visitor has fully exited the heart tunnel.

Visible:

- bright blue sky
- surrounding clouds
- two floating cherubs
- a fully closed parchment scroll centered in the composition
- open sky beneath the scroll

The proclamation text is not yet visible.

The scroll should feel like the next object waiting to be activated.

Ambient animation is already running:

- cherubs float gently
- clouds drift
- exterior atmosphere remains alive

The scroll itself remains mostly still.

---

## Closed Scroll Hold

Before the parchment begins opening, there should be a brief hold.

During this moment:

- scroll remains fully closed
- cherubs continue floating
- clouds continue drifting
- the visitor has time to register the new composition

The scene should not begin unfurling the instant it appears.

The closed state needs to read first.

---

## Scroll Opening

As the visitor begins scrolling, the parchment starts to physically unfurl.

The upper portion of the scroll remains near its established position while the lower roller begins moving downward.

As the distance between the rollers increases:

- more parchment becomes visible
- the proclamation begins appearing
- decorative elements reveal naturally
- the scroll grows vertically

The scroll should feel like a physical object opening.

It should not look like one long parchment image simply scaling taller.

---

## Pinned Reveal

During the initial opening phase, the viewport remains pinned.

The page does not immediately travel downward.

Instead:

- the sky remains fixed
- cherubs remain visible above
- clouds continue their ambient movement
- the scroll expands downward inside the viewport

The visitor should feel like their scrolling is **opening the parchment**.

This is the primary interaction language at the beginning of the scene.

---

## Proclamation Reveal

As the parchment opens, the message becomes visible progressively from top to bottom.

The reveal is created primarily by the physical unfurling of the scroll.

The proclamation should not need a separate line-by-line fade animation.

The text is revealed because the paper itself is being exposed.

The visitor should gradually encounter:

- opening declaration
- supporting statements
- decorative separators
- Ten Grand message
- final proclamation language

---

## Viewport Transition

Eventually, the parchment becomes taller than the visible viewport.

At this point, the interaction changes.

Before this threshold:

**The viewport stays still while the parchment grows.**

After this threshold:

**The visitor begins traveling down the expanded parchment.**

The change should feel seamless.

There should not be an obvious switch between animation and normal scrolling.

---

## Proclamation Journey

Once the scroll exceeds the viewport:

- the top of the parchment begins moving upward
- the visitor follows the document downward
- additional proclamation sections pass through the screen
- clouds continue framing the sides
- the scroll remains the dominant object

The visitor should be able to read the declaration naturally as they move through it.

The movement should remain controlled enough that the proclamation never feels like it is rushing past.

---

## Fully Open State

Toward the bottom of the journey, the full proclamation has been revealed.

The final portion includes the concluding declaration and the **TEN GRAND** statement.

The lower scroll roller eventually becomes visible.

There should be a short hold once the visitor reaches the completed proclamation.

The viewer should be given time to register that the message has reached its conclusion.

---

## Scroll Closing

After the full proclamation has been revealed, continued scrolling begins rolling the parchment closed again.

The closing begins from the lower portion.

As the visitor continues:

- bottom roller moves upward
- parchment is pulled back into the roll
- lower proclamation content disappears first
- remaining content gradually disappears
- visible parchment becomes shorter

The document is being physically sealed.

It should not simply fade away.

---

## Return to Closed State

As the parchment becomes short enough to fit comfortably inside the viewport again, the scene transitions back toward a pinned-object composition.

Eventually:

- top and bottom rollers are close together
- proclamation text is no longer visible
- scroll returns to a compact closed state

The declaration has now been completed and closed.

---

## Cherub Behavior

The cherubs remain part of the exterior environment.

During the opening portion:

- they float gently
- they frame the scroll
- they remain secondary to the parchment

As the visitor begins traveling down the long proclamation, they may naturally leave the top of the viewport.

They do not need to follow the entire parchment journey.

Their purpose is to frame the ceremonial opening.

---

## Cloud Behavior

Clouds continue as subtle ambient framing elements.

Possible movement includes:

- slow horizontal drift
- slight vertical breathing
- independent timing between cloud groups

The clouds should never interfere with text readability.

They should support the proclamation rather than compete with it.

---

## Ambient Animation

Ambient animation throughout Scene 6 may include:

- cherubs floating
- clouds drifting
- subtle parchment texture movement
- faint environmental light movement

The proclamation itself should remain readable and visually stable.

The primary movement is the physical opening and closing of the scroll.

---

## Scroll Behavior

Scene 6 uses three distinct scroll states within one continuous sequence.

### 1. Pinned Unfurl

The viewport remains fixed while the parchment opens.

### 2. Proclamation Travel

Once the parchment exceeds the viewport height, the viewer begins moving down the document.

### 3. Closing

After the message is complete, the parchment rolls closed again.

The complete choreography is documented in the **Scene 6 Transition** page.

---

## Ending State

By the end of Scene 6:

- the full proclamation has been revealed
- the visitor has reached the end of the declaration
- the parchment has rolled closed again
- proclamation text is no longer visible
- the closed scroll remains briefly in the exterior world
- the next scene begins emerging from below

The closed-scroll state acts as the final bridge into the finale.

## Scene Handoff

Scene 6 ends with the proclamation fully closed again.

The message has been delivered and sealed.

The closed scroll then begins clearing the frame as the final scene emerges from below.

The visitor is now ready to enter the last chapter of the Heart Direction experience.
