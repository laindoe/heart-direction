# Scene 4: Full Details

# Overall Behavior

Scene 4 begins as a direct continuation of the arrow’s movement from Scene 3.

The arrow has already passed behind the three medallions and is continuing downward.

At the same time, an unlit heart begins moving upward from below the viewport.

The arrow and heart move toward one another until they meet.

The impact triggers the central transformation of the scene:

- the arrow lodges inside the heart
- the heart bursts with light
- the dormant Quest marquee sign powers on
- the scene shifts from motion into interaction

After the visitor has had time to absorb the reveal and optionally open The Quest modal, continued scrolling carries the heart’s light downward toward the Back to Reality sign.

This scene should feel like a culmination and a release.

The arrow has been the moving guide through the previous scenes. Here, that movement is converted into illumination.

---

# Starting Frame

The starting frame is inherited directly from the ending of Scene 3.

Visible at the beginning:

- arrow traveling downward
- medallion scene moving out above
- lower tunnel environment beginning to appear
- heart beginning to rise from below

At this point:

- the heart is unlit
- the heart rays are inactive
- the Quest sign is already attached across the heart
- the Quest sign is dormant
- the Quest bulbs or light border are off or very dim
- the words “THE QUEST” are visible but subdued

The arrow remains vertically centered above the heart.

There should be no cut or reset between Scene 3 and Scene 4.

---

# 1. Scene Environment

The environment should remain visually focused around the central vertical path.

The main visual axis is:

Arrow

↓

Heart

↓

Lower tunnel

↓

Back to Reality

The scene should be simpler than the medallion section so the heart activation has enough visual space to feel important.

The heart is the dominant object.

The Quest sign becomes the secondary focal point after activation.

---

# 2. Background

The background should remain quiet and dark enough that the heart’s light can create a meaningful contrast.

Possible elements:

- dark tunnel atmosphere
- subtle grain
- faint texture
- small stars or decorative marks
- soft haze
- low-level environmental glow

## Idle Behavior

Before impact, the background remains mostly static.

Optional ambient movement:

- extremely slow texture drift
- subtle opacity breathing
- faint haze movement

The background should not brighten significantly before the heart activates.

---

# 3. Cloud Structure

Clouds can continue framing the scene from both sides.

Recommended groups:

- upper-left clouds
- upper-right clouds
- middle-left clouds
- middle-right clouds
- lower clouds near the heart
- lower clouds near the tunnel exit

The clouds should create depth without blocking the central story line.

They should leave open space around:

- arrow
- heart
- Quest sign
- light rays
- Back to Reality sign

---

# 4. Cloud Idle Animation

Cloud movement should remain subtle.

Possible movement:

- slow horizontal drift
- small vertical float
- very gentle scale breathing

Suggested ranges:

- horizontal: approximately 2 to 6 pixels
- vertical: approximately 2 to 5 pixels
- scale: approximately 1 to 1.01
- duration: approximately 10 to 18 seconds

Avoid:

- clouds crossing the arrow path
- clouds covering the Quest sign
- clouds blocking the Back to Reality reveal
- synchronized motion

---

# 5. Arrow

The same arrow from Scenes 2 and 3 continues into Scene 4.

It should not visually reset or be replaced by a new object.

The viewer should perceive one continuous arrow journey.

The arrow remains vertically centered.

---

# 6. Arrow Movement Into Scene 4

As the visitor scrolls:

- arrow continues downward
- arrow remains straight
- arrow does not rotate
- arrow does not drift horizontally
- arrow maintains the same visual scale and material appearance

The medallions are left behind above.

The arrow should move only as far as necessary to meet the rising heart.

---

# 7. Heart

The heart begins below the visible viewport and moves upward toward the arrow.

Before impact, the heart is in a dormant state.

It should already contain the Quest sign, but neither the heart nor the sign should yet appear fully activated.

The heart should feel like a destination waiting to be awakened.

---

# 8. Heart Pre-Impact State

Before the arrow reaches it:

- heart is visible but comparatively dark
- internal glow is off
- outer glow is off or extremely faint
- radial rays are inactive
- Quest marquee is dormant
- Quest bulbs are off or barely lit

The heart should remain readable, but the full visual payoff is reserved for impact.

---

# 9. Heart Movement

The heart moves upward as the arrow moves downward.

The two movements should be coordinated so the objects visibly approach one another.

The heart should remain horizontally centered beneath the arrow.

Avoid:

- large side-to-side motion
- spinning
- dramatic rotation
- bouncing

The movement should feel intentional and ceremonial.

---

# 10. Impact Alignment

The arrow should strike near the upper-center area of the heart.

The alignment should make the impact immediately readable.

The arrow should not appear to graze the side.

The heart should not need to shift horizontally at the last second.

The composition should already be aligned before impact.

---

# 11. Arrow Impact

When the arrow reaches the heart:

- arrowhead enters behind the heart artwork
- a small portion of the shaft also disappears behind the heart
- arrow stops
- heart activation begins immediately

The arrow should appear physically lodged in the heart.

Do not:

- stop the arrow on top of the heart
- fade the arrow out
- let the arrow pass completely through
- bounce the arrow backward

---

# 12. Arrow Masking

The arrow impact will likely require masking or layer management.

Possible techniques:

- CSS clip-path
- overflow masking container
- SVG mask
- split arrow layers
- dedicated impact mask

The simplest reliable method should be used.

The final visual should show:

- visible shaft above heart
- arrow entering heart
- hidden arrowhead portion
- heart artwork visually in front

---

# 13. Heart Activation Trigger

The heart’s light should activate only after contact.

The impact is the cause.

The visual order should be:

1. Arrow touches heart
2. Arrow enters heart
3. Heart flashes
4. Heart glow expands
5. Quest sign powers on

The sequence should feel immediate but still readable.

---

# 14. Heart Light Burst

The heart activation should be one of the strongest light events in the tunnel sequence.

Possible components:

- bright internal flash
- outer halo
- radial rays
- glow bloom
- subtle light spilling into nearby clouds
- short atmospheric brightness shift

The burst should feel more dramatic than the medallion glows or Scene 1 marquee flicker.

This is a major story beat.

---

# 15. Heart Light Structure

The heart should ideally be separated into multiple visual layers:

### Heart Base

The original illustrated heart.

### Inner Glow

Brighter version inside the heart.

### Outer Halo

Soft glow around the perimeter.

### Radial Rays

Light beams extending outward.

### Bloom Layer

Optional larger soft flash used only during activation.

Separating these elements allows the activation to feel dimensional.

---

# 16. Heart Activation Sequence

Recommended sequence:

### Step 1

Arrow enters heart.

### Step 2

Very brief bright flash at impact point.

### Step 3

Inner glow expands through the heart.

### Step 4

Outer halo grows outward.

### Step 5

Radial light rays appear.

### Step 6

Quest sign powers on.

### Step 7

Heart settles into an illuminated idle state.

The whole activation should feel fast enough to be satisfying but not so fast that the viewer misses it.

---

# 17. Heart Lit Idle State

After activation, the heart remains illuminated.

Its idle behavior can include:

- gentle breathing brightness
- soft halo fluctuation
- very subtle ray movement
- occasional light shimmer

The heart should not return to its original dormant state while the visitor remains in the scene.

---

# 18. The Quest Sign

The Quest is a marquee-style ribbon sign attached across the heart.

It should visually connect to the Enter the Tunnel sign from Scene 1.

The sign should feel hand-drawn and theatrical rather than like standard UI.

The entire sign functions as a button.

---

# 19. Quest Pre-Impact State

Before the arrow hits:

- sign is already physically present
- frame is visible
- bulbs are off or extremely dim
- lettering is visible but low contrast
- sign has little or no glow
- no pulse occurs
- sign should not yet read as the main focal point

It should feel dormant.

---

# 20. Quest Activation

The heart’s activation powers on the Quest sign.

When the arrow hits:

- Quest bulbs illuminate
- lettering becomes bright
- outer sign glow appears
- border or trim may catch light
- sign gives one subtle emphasis pulse

The reveal should feel like a marquee switching on.

---

# 21. Quest Light-Up Sequence

Recommended order:

1. heart impact
2. heart flash
3. Quest bulbs switch on
4. “THE QUEST” lettering brightens
5. sign glow expands
6. sign gives one small pulse
7. sign settles into lit state

The sign can illuminate:

- all at once
- or from center outward

Either way, it should feel intentional.

---

# 22. Quest Bulb Behavior

Once activated, the bulbs should remain mostly illuminated.

Possible idle behavior:

- soft steady glow
- occasional subtle flicker
- gentle brightness breathing
- restrained marquee shimmer

Avoid:

- constant chasing lights
- full blackouts
- rapid blinking
- synchronized strobing

The sign should remain easy to read.

---

# 23. Quest Sign Interactive State

The sign should clearly communicate that it can be clicked or tapped.

On desktop hover:

- bulbs brighten
- outer glow strengthens
- lettering brightens slightly
- optional scale increase

Suggested scale:

approximately 1.02 to 1.04

On mobile touch:

- sign brightens immediately
- short highlight flash
- modal opens

The sign should not bounce.

---

# 24. Quest Hit Area

The entire sign should function as the interaction target.

The invisible hit area should include:

- sign body
- marquee border
- reasonable outer tap padding

The clickable area should not require the visitor to tap the text precisely.

# 25. Quest Modal

Tapping the Quest sign opens its modal.

The modal content is separate from the scene animation, but the behavior should remain consistent with the previous modal system.

The modal should feel like an information layer within the world.

---

# 26. Modal Opening Behavior

When the Quest sign is selected:

- sign briefly brightens
- scroll progress pauses
- modal backdrop appears
- modal content opens above the scene
- heart remains illuminated behind it
- arrow remains lodged inside heart

The underlying scene should remain preserved.

---

# 27. Modal Closing Behavior

When the modal closes:

- backdrop disappears
- modal disappears
- visitor returns to same scroll position
- heart remains lit
- arrow remains lodged
- Quest sign remains active
- scene does not replay

The visitor can reopen The Quest if desired.

---

# 28. Scene Hold

After the heart activates, the scene should enter a deliberate hold.

During the hold:

- arrow remains stationary
- heart stays illuminated
- Quest sign remains lit
- Quest remains clickable
- heart glow breathes
- clouds continue subtle movement
- no major scroll-driven transition begins immediately

This is the primary interactive moment of Scene 4.

---

# 29. Light as a Transition Device

After the hold, the heart’s light becomes the guide toward the tunnel exit.

This light should visually connect the heart to the Back to Reality sign below.

The light should not appear as a separate unrelated effect.

It should feel like the activation of the heart is now illuminating the path forward.

---

# 30. Downward Light Travel

As the visitor continues scrolling:

- light extends downward from the heart
- lower tunnel area begins brightening
- glow gradually occupies more of the bottom of the viewport
- light intensity increases nearer the exit
- heart remains visible above for part of the transition

The light should move visually downward through the tunnel.

---

# 31. Light Structure

The downward light may be built from:

- vertical glow layer
- soft gradient beam
- illuminated haze
- light rays
- environmental brightness mask

It should not look like a hard rectangular spotlight.

The effect should feel atmospheric.

---

# 32. Back to Reality Sign

The Back to Reality sign is the destination at the bottom of Scene 4.

It should not appear abruptly.

Before the light reaches it, the sign may be:

- hidden
- very dark
- low contrast
- partially obscured

As the heart light travels downward, the sign becomes increasingly visible.

---

# 33. Back to Reality Reveal

The reveal should happen progressively.

Possible sequence:

1. faint silhouette of sign becomes visible
2. edges catch light
3. lettering becomes readable
4. sign reaches full visibility
5. tunnel exit beneath or behind it becomes clearer

The reveal should feel like the heart’s light is physically exposing the way out.

---

# 34. Visual Attention Hierarchy

Before impact:

1. arrow
2. heart
3. Quest sign dormant
4. clouds

At impact:

1. heart light burst
2. arrow lodged in heart
3. Quest sign activation

During hold:

1. Quest sign
2. illuminated heart
3. arrow
4. ambient environment

During exit transition:

1. downward-moving light
2. Back to Reality sign
3. heart receding above

---

# 35. Ambient Animation Hierarchy

## Primary Ambient Motion

- heart glow breathing
- Quest marquee light behavior

## Secondary Ambient Motion

- clouds
- subtle rays
- atmospheric glow

## Still Elements

- arrow after impact
- heart structure
- Quest sign structure
- Back to Reality sign once fully revealed

---

# 37. Camera Behavior

The camera remains aligned to the central vertical story path.

No dramatic side-to-side movement is needed.

The sense of movement comes from:

- arrow traveling down
- heart moving up
- light extending down
- Back to Reality appearing below

The camera should feel stable enough for interaction.

---

# 38. Scroll Behavior

Scene 4 contains three scroll states.

## Impact State

Arrow and heart move toward each other until contact.

## Interactive Hold

Heart remains lit and Quest remains available.

## Exit Preparation

Light travels downward and reveals Back to Reality.

The transition out should remain controlled and readable.

---

# 39. Reverse Scroll Behavior

If the visitor reverses before impact:

- heart moves back down
- arrow moves back up
- heart returns to dormant state
- Quest sign returns to dormant state

If the visitor reverses after impact:

- arrow should visually pull back out
- heart illumination should reduce
- Quest lights should turn off
- scene should reconnect cleanly with the previous state

This should be tuned carefully so the effect does not flash abruptly.

---

# 40. Modal + Scroll Lock

While The Quest modal is open:

- scroll timeline should not advance
- arrow position should remain fixed
- heart lighting should remain stable
- Back to Reality transition should not begin

Closing the modal restores normal scrolling.

---

# 41. Mobile Behavior

Mobile portrait is the master composition.

The viewport should clearly show:

- arrow approaching heart
- heart impact
- Quest sign
- heart light burst
- lower light path

The Quest sign should remain large enough to tap comfortably.

Its hit area should be at least approximately 44 × 44 CSS pixels.

The Back to Reality reveal should remain readable even with changing browser bar height.

---

# 42. Mobile Modal Behavior

On mobile:

- modal should fit within safe viewport
- modal content may scroll internally
- underlying page remains locked
- close control stays easy to reach
- closing preserves exact scene position

---

# 43. Desktop Behavior

Desktop should preserve the same choreography.

Wider screens may reveal more:

- clouds
- light rays
- tunnel atmosphere

The central relationship remains:

arrow → heart → Quest → Back to Reality

Do not move the Quest sign away from the heart on desktop.

---

# 44. Performance Requirements

Use transform and opacity for primary movement.

Prefer pre-rendered glow assets for:

- heart halo
- Quest glow
- downward light
- bloom effects

Avoid constantly animating large real-time blur filters if possible.

The heart activation can combine:

- scale
- opacity
- pre-rendered glow layers

This will be lighter on mobile.

---

# 45. Quest Light Performance

A lightweight marquee implementation can use:

- bulb overlay image
- opacity animation
- optional small brightness variation
- limited number of independently animated groups

The sign does not need dozens of individually rendered DOM bulbs unless the final design specifically requires it.

---

# 46. Reduced Motion

For visitors using `prefers-reduced-motion`:

- shorten arrow/heart travel
- simplify impact flash
- reduce radial ray movement
- keep Quest sign activation clear
- reduce cloud movement
- reveal Back to Reality with a shorter light fade

The narrative sequence should remain:

arrow reaches heart → heart activates → Quest lights up → exit is revealed.

---

- heart outer glow

- Quest glow

- Back to Reality sign

The main mechanics to solve before final illustration are:

- heart activation timing
- Quest power-on sequence
- Quest modal preservation

- Back to Reality transition

---

# 48. Locked Scene Behavior

When Scene 4 begins:

- arrow continues downward from Scene 3
- unlit heart rises from below

- arrow lodges inside heart

# Acceptance Criteria

Scene 4 is complete when:

1. The arrow continues seamlessly from Scene 3.
2. The heart enters from below in a dormant state.
3. The Quest sign is already present before activation.
4. The Quest sign remains visually subdued before impact.
5. Arrow and heart remain perfectly aligned.
6. Arrow appears to physically enter the heart.
7. Arrow stops after impact.
8. Heart activation begins only after contact.
9. Heart produces a clear light burst.
10. Heart settles into a stable illuminated state.
11. Quest sign powers on as part of the heart activation.
12. Quest bulbs illuminate clearly.
13. “THE QUEST” becomes bright and readable.
14. Quest sign gives one subtle emphasis pulse.
15. Quest sign remains lit after activation.
16. Quest clearly reads as clickable.
17. Tapping Quest opens the modal.
18. Opening the modal preserves the scene.
19. Closing the modal preserves exact scroll position.
20. Arrow remains lodged while modal is open.
21. Heart remains illuminated while modal is open.
22. Quest can be reopened.
23. Opening the modal is optional.
24. There is a clear scene hold after activation.
25. Heart glow continues subtly during the hold.
26. Continued scrolling begins extending light downward.
27. Downward light clearly originates from the activated heart.
28. Lower tunnel brightens progressively.
29. Back to Reality appears gradually rather than suddenly.
30. Back to Reality becomes the new visual focal point.
31. Clouds never obscure the arrow impact or Quest sign.
32. Reverse scroll returns the scene cleanly toward its dormant state.
33. No masking errors appear around the arrow/heart impact.
34. Light effects remain smooth on mobile.
35. Quest interaction remains easy to use on mobile.
36. Final artwork can replace placeholders without rebuilding the scene architecture.
37. The scene is ready to hand off into the separate **Back to Reality transition**.
